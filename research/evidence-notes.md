# Supplement evidence decisions

Reviewed 10 October 2026. This is an evidence reference for generally healthy adults, with safety and interactions handled by the separate safety research. Model dose units must not be presented as instructions to manufacture supplements.

Use two automatic **reference** candidates only when all visible assumptions hold: creatine monohydrate 3 g/day for Strength with regular relevant training, and caffeine 75 mg in one morning serving for acute Focus/Energy (alertness). Caffeine is vetoed when Sleep or Calm is prioritized, timing or other intake is unknown, or the safety policy excludes it. The screen must describe its assumptions because it does not collect a personal profile. A zero-ball result is valid.

At 250 mg creatine per ball, require twelve balls to reach 3 g. Do not treat 0.25–2.75 g as a complete evidence-based serving. The no-loading protocol operates over roughly 3–4 weeks; today's twelve balls do not instantly increase strength. The supported claim is relevant training capacity/adaptation. Energy in this model means caffeine-related acute alertness, not persistent-fatigue treatment. [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/)

At 25 mg caffeine per ball, three units represent **one** 75 mg serving. EFSA's evidence review retained 75 mg because most tested outcomes below that amount were null. Caffeine can improve alertness and some reaction-time measures; this model gives no Memory or Strength coverage at 75 mg. Morning timing and the 8-hour margin are conservative reference rules, not guarantees about an individual's sleep. The 400 mg all-source daily and 200 mg single-intake safety references are ceilings, not targets. [EFSA efficacy opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3574), [75/150 mg trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15678363/), [EFSA safety](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine)

Withhold L-theanine from automatic routine planning. The July 2026 meta-analysis supports a specific **single 200 mg dose, 30–60 minutes before testing**, for choice reaction-time performance. It does not establish sustained productivity or a daily chronic protocol. Stress reduction was modest and substantially influenced by high-bias trials; anxiety findings were inconsistent and fatigue was not improved. The review reports no serious events in included trials, which does not settle long-term safety or interactions. COI: coauthor Guillaume Fond founded a company selling dietary supplements. PubMed abstract and conflict statement were inspected; the publisher full text was unavailable. [Gerolymos et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42410082/)

Theanine's sleep overlap remains insufficient to score. Its 2025 synthesis found subjective signals but no significant objective sleep improvements; many included products combined ingredients, and an adequate pure-theanine dose and duration remain unresolved. [Bulman et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079225000292)

Do not assign an effective magnesium sleep dose. The August 2026 synthesis of 12 main sleep trials rates certainty low to very low and does not support routine insomnia use. An illustrative 50 mg elemental-magnesium ball should remain excluded, not become an effective dose because six balls fit a common nutrition range. Older-adult evidence does not establish benefit in a nondeficient healthy adult. [2026 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42661485/), [2021 older-adult review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865376/)

Creatine's proposed memory overlap is uncertain. An older review had a stronger older-adult subgroup signal and a null younger subgroup, while EFSA did not establish a general cognition claim. Later methodological commentary raises concerns about pooling nonindependent outcomes. Do not optimize a creatine Memory plan or imply that the 3 g training dose is an established memory dose. [Memory review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35984306/), [EFSA cognition opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9100), [2026 methodological commentary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42039906/)

Protein is conditional on assessed food intake and training; a fixed 20 g supplement is not universally necessary. Glycine's 3 g bedtime studies are small and high-bias, so exclude it. Melatonin belongs to specific timing/circadian situations and should not automatically cover a general Sleep priority. [Protein review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/), [Glycine review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10828290/), [NCCIH sleep guidance](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sleep-disorders-and-complementary-health-approaches)

Sleep, Memory and Calm can remain uncovered. No assessed candidate earns broad automatic coverage for these labels under the screen's missing clinical and timing context. Any geometry or optimization score is a design heuristic; it is not a percentage improvement or probability of success. Individual ingredient studies do not validate the combined stack.
