# Evidence and limits for the playable blend

Reviewed on 2026-10-10. This document supports the revised four-ingredient playable model. It does not replace a clinical assessment or establish that a selected combination benefits a particular person.

## Model boundary

The model assumes a generally healthy, nonpregnant adult with no kidney disease, regular medication, other supplements, or other caffeine, and early-day caffeine use. Those are reference assumptions, not verified facts about the visitor. Keep the compact interface statement that health, medicines, and current intake have not been checked.

Numeric ingredient–goal weights are **gameplay affinities** chosen to create useful overlap and satisfying tradeoffs. They are not measured clinical effect sizes, benefit percentages, or probabilities. Battery levels are preferences; they are not diagnoses or nutrient needs. A ball represents a quantity, not an experimentally validated unit of benefit. Intermediate quantities below a studied dose must not be described as effective doses.

Sleep/Calm versus caffeine locks are **gameplay balancing rules** motivated by caffeine's potential to disturb sleep or cause jitteriness. A particular battery cutoff is not a universal medical contradiction. High Sleep and high Focus can coexist through nonstimulant routes. L-theanine or magnesium must not be presented as neutralizing caffeine's sleep effects or making stimulant exposure safe.

## Quantities and limits

| Ingredient | One ball | Maximum generated daily quantity | Meaning of the maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | 250 mg | 3,000 mg, 12 balls | Conservative application cap; common daily training reference, not an established population UL |
| Caffeine | 25 mg | 75 mg, 3 balls | Conservative application cap; not a formal nutrient UL or daily requirement |
| L-theanine | 50 mg | 200 mg, 4 balls | Application cap informed by short-term trials; no established UL identified |
| Magnesium | 50 mg **elemental magnesium** | 200 mg elemental magnesium, 4 balls | Application cap below the US 350 mg supplemental UL and the narrower European 250 mg limit |

Caps apply across the entire blend. Multiple goals share the same ingredient quantity and cannot multiply its cap. Real exposure accounting must include other products according to the limit's actual scope. The interface does not measure those exposures and must not claim personal safety clearance.

## Goal associations

| Goal | Ingredient associations | Evidence interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | L-theanine; magnesium | Exploratory. Small theanine trials report selected subjective sleep improvements. Generic magnesium has insufficient rigorous evidence for a general sleep recommendation. |
| Memory | Creatine; L-theanine | Exploratory and task-specific. Creatine cognitive findings vary by population and dose. A theanine trial found acute working-memory-task improvements; this does not establish lasting memory enhancement. |
| Focus | Caffeine; L-theanine; creatine | Caffeine has established acute attention evidence. Theanine has preliminary task-level evidence. Creatine is a deliberately exploratory cognitive association; a focus benefit at the model's 3 g cap is not established. |
| Strength | Creatine; magnesium | Creatine has substantial training/performance evidence. Magnesium supports normal muscle function and nutritional adequacy; extra magnesium is not established to enhance strength in adequately nourished adults. |
| Calm | L-theanine; magnesium | Exploratory. Stress/anxiety results are limited and population-dependent; these are not treatments for anxiety disorders. |
| Energy | Caffeine; creatine; magnesium | Distinguish alertness from physical energy availability and normal nutrient metabolism. None establishes treatment of persistent fatigue or substitutes for sleep. |

## Supporting primary research and authoritative assessments

**Creatine.** [NIH ODS exercise and athletic performance guidance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/) describes muscle energy supply during short, intense activity, strength/training benefits, and 3–5 g maintenance amounts. Three grams daily without loading is a conservative training reference. No numeric population UL is established by this guidance. Water-related weight gain and gastrointestinal discomfort can occur; kidney disease requires individual professional review. Evidence in healthy adults does not justify claiming that customary creatine doses damage healthy kidneys.

For the Memory association, [Rae et al., 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1691485/) studied 45 young adult vegetarians using **5 g/day for six weeks**, reporting better working-memory and reasoning-task performance. This is neither a general-population proof nor a demonstration of efficacy at 3 g/day. [Sandkühler et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10647179/) offers a larger randomized cognitive study; together the trials support a cautious, emerging association, not guaranteed cognitive improvement.

**Caffeine.** [EFSA's attention/alertness assessment](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/3574) supports an acute association at 75 mg. [Dodd et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25761837/) directly studied 75 mg caffeine and found attention and mood effects in a small crossover trial. It also illustrates that caffeine–theanine combinations do not reliably produce additive benefits. Do not apply the 75 mg claim automatically to every intermediate 25 mg ball.

[FDA caffeine guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) describes 400 mg/day as an amount generally not associated with negative effects for most adults, with substantial individual variation. [EFSA safety guidance](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/caffeine) assesses 200 mg single doses and 400 mg/day for healthy nonpregnant adults; these are all-source assessment levels, not targets. Sensitivity, medicines, and other stimulants matter. [NHLBI healthy sleep guidance](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/healthy-sleep-habits) notes that caffeine effects can last up to eight hours. Early timing is a conservative model assumption, not a guarantee against sleep disturbance. Use quantified finished products; never suggest measuring bulk pure caffeine powder.

**L-theanine.** [Baba et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8080935/), DOI [10.1089/jmf.2020.4803](https://doi.org/10.1089/jmf.2020.4803), tested 100.6 mg in middle-aged/older participants with self-assessed cognitive decline. A single dose improved selected attention and working-memory-task results. Retain the restricted, exploratory interpretation. [Hidese et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6836118/), DOI [10.3390/nu11102362](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11102362), used 200 mg/day for four weeks in a 30-person crossover trial. Selected subjective sleep subscales favored theanine, but several overall between-treatment comparisons were nonsignificant or trend-level. There were no apparent adverse events in that small trial.

[FDA's 2024 briefing review](https://www.fda.gov/media/182086/download) reports generally favorable short-term oral tolerability but important reporting gaps: 19 studies included 554 healthy adults at 50–600 mg/day for one day to six weeks; 13 did not discuss adverse events. That does not establish a safe long-term daily ceiling. No established UL was identified. Food GRAS status is not an efficacy endorsement or unrestricted supplement safety clearance. Pregnancy, medicine use, blood-pressure context, and combination use remain unassessed. Do not invent a clinically proven pairwise interaction or claim that theanine cancels caffeine.

**Magnesium.** [NIH ODS magnesium guidance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/) supports normal energy, nerve, and muscle function. The US adult UL is **350 mg/day from supplements and medicines**, excluding naturally occurring food magnesium. Diarrhea and nausea are possible; impaired kidney function increases toxicity risk. Oral bisphosphonates require at least two hours separation. Tetracycline/quinolone antibiotics should be taken at least two hours before or four to six hours after magnesium; actual schedules require professional review. Diuretics and proton-pump inhibitors can affect magnesium status.

[The European magnesium assessment](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/efsa_rep/blobserver_assets/ndatolerableuil.pdf) sets **250 mg/day** for readily dissociable salts and compounds such as magnesium oxide in supplements, water, or added food/drinks, excluding naturally present food magnesium. The 200 mg application cap leaves some headroom but cannot account for unknown other products.

[NCCIH's sleep evidence assessment](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/in-the-news-magnesium-supplements-for-sleep-disorders) finds too little rigorous evidence to determine general effectiveness. For Calm, [Tarleton et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28654669/) used 248 mg elemental magnesium in adults with depression symptoms; the trial was open-label and used a no-treatment control. It is a preliminary, indirect association for this healthy-adult model, not proof of calm enhancement at 200 mg. Findings from magnesium L-threonate trials must not be transferred to this generic elemental-magnesium ingredient or used to add a generic magnesium Memory claim.

## Required behavior contracts

- An accepted priority increase retains every existing ingredient quantity. It may add balls or leave quantities unchanged when benefits overlap or caps bind.
- Validate conflict and cap rules before accepting an increase. A blocked control leaves priorities, quantities, and existing balls unchanged.
- Only an explicit priority decrease can remove balls. Decreasing one goal retains quantities still supported by another goal.
- Alternative associations create route options within fixed ingredient caps; they do not establish additive clinical efficacy or permission to exceed a limit.
- Describe the result as a **playable reference blend** or **exploratory blend**, and reserve strong benefit language for supported associations. Keep source and qualification information in the existing evidence presentation rather than adding interface panels.
